# Animal light references for dark prototype E

For designers comparing quiet ocean backgrounds for Skyttel's household map.
Source review: 2026-10-16. Dark E works for the user; this note supplies
the references for six additional animal forms requested for comparison.
See [the ocean study](./ocean-background.md) for light-theme references.

## What produces the light

Bioluminescence is light from a chemical reaction. Light can come from animal
tissues, specialized organs called photophores, or bacteria living with an
animal. Some animals release luminous material into the surrounding water.
These mechanisms give different visual references: localized dots, a luminous
body region, a separate lure, or a detached cloud.
[WHOI: How does bioluminescence work?](https://www.whoi.edu/ocean-learning-hub/ocean-facts/how-does-bioluminescence-work/)
[MBARI: Deep-sea anglerfish](https://www.mbari.org/animal/deep-sea-anglerfish/)

Do not imply that every marine animal glows, or that luminous animals remain
lit continuously. Their emissions can serve defense, camouflage, hunting or
courtship. Most marine bioluminescence is blue-green; other colors exist.
[Smithsonian: Bioluminescence](https://ocean.si.edu/ocean-life/fish/bioluminescence)

## Six useful animal references

### Crown jelly: a ring with radial structure

**Observed reference:** MBARI describes blue pinwheel-like alarm displays in
deep-sea crown jellies, *Atolla* spp. Their bell is normally red and concealed
in deep water. The luminous display is a defensive response, not a permanent
blue outline. Most have one unusually long trailing tentacle; this feature
does not apply to every species.
[MBARI: Deep-sea crown jelly](https://www.mbari.org/animal/deep-sea-crown-jelly/)

**Design inference:** use a faint scalloped crown or broken blue ring, with a
barely visible bell and one fine trailing curve. Freeze an illustrative
instant rather than reproducing the rotating alarm. This gives a readable
marine silhouette with little bright area.

### Siphonophore: a segmented curved ribbon

**Observed reference:** MBARI documents blue bioluminescence from
*Frillagalma vityazi*, with paired photographs under external illumination
and while emitting its own light. Siphonophores are colonies of specialized
units called zooids, rather than single jellyfish with ordinary tentacles.
[MBARI: Light-producing deep-sea animals](https://www.mbari.org/news/new-study-shows-that-three-quarters-of-deep-sea-animals-make-their-own-light/)
[Smithsonian: Jellyfish and Comb Jellies](https://ocean.si.edu/ocean-life/invertebrates/jellyfish-and-comb-jellies)

**Design inference:** a gently bending chain of small translucent units with
selective blue highlights provides a long form to contrast with round bells.
The exact glowing segments would be an illustration, not an anatomical map.
Do not assume that all siphonophores emit the same color or pattern.

### Lanternfish: light arranged along a body

**Observed reference:** lanternfishes have light-producing organs along the
belly and sides. WHOI describes blue-green lights and arrangements that vary
among species. Ventral illumination helps disguise the silhouette against
light from above, a strategy called counterillumination. Possible signaling
functions should not be presented as a fully decoded language.
[WHOI: Lanternfishes](https://www.whoi.edu/ocean-learning-hub/ocean-facts/lanternfishes/)

**Design inference:** put a short, slightly curved row of tiny cyan dots on a
very faint fish silhouette. Keep it clearly attached to a body so it does
not resemble household objects connected in a graph. Avoid inventing a
species-specific pattern and presenting it as scientifically exact.

### Vampire squid: arm-tip lights and a detached cloud

**Observed reference:** *Vampyroteuthis infernalis* can produce blue light at
its arm tips when threatened and release luminous fluid to distract a
predator. Despite its common name, it is not a true squid. These are
different light placements: small tips on an animal and material in water.
[MBARI: Vampire squid](https://www.mbari.org/animal/vampire-squid/)

**Design inference:** a dim webbed silhouette with a few separated cyan tips
is distinctive. A separate diffuse speckled cloud is a possible alternative,
but should not fill the map or suggest that ordinary marine snow glows.

### Ostracod: a trail of secreted light points

**Observed reference:** males of the Caribbean ostracod
*Photeros annecohenae* produce blue courtship displays resembling dotted
trains. The behavioral study uses the earlier name *Vargula annecohenae*
and describes pulses of luminous secretion released into the water. This
is a shallow seagrass reference, not evidence of a deep-ocean resident.
[Smithsonian: You Light Up My World!](https://ocean.si.edu/ocean-life/invertebrates/you-light-my-world)
[Original courtship study](https://doi.org/10.1242/jeb.011130)

**Design inference:** a few short, unevenly spaced dotted trails could be
shown as frozen traces. They should be secondary accents: extensive dotted
paths could look like relationships in the household map.

### Deep-sea anglerfish: a separate bacterial lure

**Observed reference:** luminous deep-sea anglerfish lures contain glowing
bacteria in the tip structure called the esca. The appendage attracts prey
while the fish remains dark. This does not mean every anglerfish has a
luminous lure; MBARI distinguishes nonluminous seafloor anglers.
[MBARI: Deep-sea anglerfish](https://www.mbari.org/animal/deep-sea-anglerfish/)

**Design inference:** one small light at the end of a fine curved filament
could suggest the mechanism. This is a weaker first choice: an isolated
bright point can compete with selectable map objects, and a recognizably
predatory face may change the mood of the interface.

## Comb-jelly distinction

Comb jellies also offer rounded or lobed silhouettes. Some can emit their
own blue or green light, but the familiar moving rainbow along their comb
rows is scattered external light. For a self-luminous dark scene, use a
restrained blue-green treatment rather than labeling rainbow stripes as
bioluminescence. MBARI confirms that *Mnemiopsis leidyi* and
*Bolinopsis infundibulum* can synthesize their own light-emitting compound.
[Smithsonian: Jellyfish and Comb Jellies](https://ocean.si.edu/ocean-life/invertebrates/jellyfish-and-comb-jellies)
[MBARI: Comb jellies make their own glowing compounds](https://www.mbari.org/news/glow-your-own-comb-jellies-make-their-own-glowing-compounds-instead-of-getting-them-from-food/)

## Prototype composition

The user requests all six forms. Offer the combined composition and each
form individually, alongside the original dark E jellyfish for comparison.
The crown, segmented colony and fish with light rows provide complementary
silhouettes; arm-tip lights with fluid, secretion trails and a bacterial
lure add different light placements. This composition does not claim that
these animals form one observed community. Scales, positions and colors
are freely composed for the interface.

Use sparse blue and cyan accents near the edges with a dark, quiet center.
Freeze the illustrative forms: no autonomous swimming, flashing or pulsing.
The natural timing of alarm and courtship displays is reference material,
not a requirement to animate the background. Before accepting an extension,
compare object visibility and label legibility on sparse and dense maps.
